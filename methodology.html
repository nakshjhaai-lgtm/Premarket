Methodology

Everything the dashboard shows, and how it is produced.

Schedule

Four scheduled runs cover a full day. At 08:40 IST (03:10 UTC) the news cycle collects headlines, earnings dates and event flags; at 09:00 IST (03:30 UTC) the premarket scan builds the opening bias; at 09:10 IST (03:40 UTC) the per stock engine resolves the latest United States closes and relearns every stock's own corrections (Monday to Saturday, so the Friday US close is caught on Saturday); and at 09:20 IST (03:50 UTC) a confirmation pass records what price action actually did after the bell, resolves the opening bias call and appends everything to the same report. Each typically finishes in under three minutes. The Run scan now button triggers the premarket pipeline on demand (rate limited to once per 2 minutes). Every run overwrites “latest” and appends to the dated archive; 400 days of history are kept.

The decision framework (roles, not equal votes)

Earlier versions of this tool counted green vs red indicators as ±1 votes. That was wrong, and it is gone. The four inputs measure different things and are weighted accordingly:

Step 1: establish the opening bias from GIFT Nifty

The implied gap is GIFT Nifty's level vs the previous NIFTY 50 close, and its size matters, not just its colour: below 0.15% is neutral, 0.15 to 0.5% is a modest gap, above 0.5% is a strong gap. A tiny positive move is not equivalent to a substantial one. It remains an estimate, because GIFT is a futures contract carrying basis, not a guaranteed opening price.

Step 2: check whether global markets support the bias

The previous US session's close and live US index futures (S&P, Nasdaq, Dow) are read as separate inputs, alongside the Asian session. If GIFT Nifty indicates a positive open while US futures fall and Asia is weak, the framework reports conflicting evidence rather than flipping to a bearish call. Global cues are context; they do not automatically override the domestic price discovery that GIFT Nifty and the pre open session represent.

Step 3: cross confirm with the NSE pre open session

Between 09:00 and 09:15 the NSE runs order entry and matching, publishing indicative opening levels. The framework compares that indication with GIFT Nifty's: agreement confirms the bias; disagreement produces an explicit conflicting signals, no directional conclusion classification. When the feed is unreachable (nseindia.com blocks datacenter IPs), the report says so and simply loses domestic confirmation. It never invents it.

Step 4: confirm the trend after the open

From 09:15, breadth earns its job: whether the index holds above or below its opening price, and whether advancers or decliners dominate. The 09:20 IST confirmation pass records the actual open, the last price vs open, live breadth and live VIX. These are opening bias classifications and early confirmations, not predictions of the whole session; a market can open 0.5% higher and fall all day.

Step 5: use India VIX for risk, not direction

A VIX of 18 does not mean bearish, and 14 does not mean calm in absolute terms. The report reads VIX against its own trailing 1 year distribution: below the 25th percentile is low, 25 to 60 moderate, 60 to 85 elevated, above 85 high. Elevated VIX alongside a bullish bias means “manage risk harder”, not “expect a fall”.

Two questions, kept separate

  • Question A: will the market open higher or lower? Answered by the GIFT gap, cross confirmed by the NSE pre open, contextualised by global cues. This is what the headline classification is.
  • Question B: will the move sustain after the open? Judged from the post open confirmation snapshot, live breadth, and the session's own price action. The LSTM/TimesFM forecasts are inputs to this question, with all their caveats on the model page.

Legacy score

The old ±1 equal vote score (−5 to +5) is still computed and stored in each report for archive continuity, but the dashboard and headlines lead with the framework classification above. History entries made before the change show their original scores.

Implied open

GIFT Nifty's current level minus NIFTY 50's previous close, as a percentage. Caveat, stated on every report: GIFT is a futures contract: the gap includes the futures basis (interest, dividends, time to expiry), so it is an indication of the open, not a prediction of it.

LSTM forecast

The 5 session NIFTY path and its rolling 10 session backtest come from the 3 layer LSTM described on the model page. The forecast panel additionally guards itself: if the last 60 scaled price points fall outside [−0.05, 1.05] of the frozen training scaler, the panel displays an unreliable, retrain now warning instead of pretending the numbers are valid.

Per stock adaptive engine (every symbol learns)

Since October 2026 every stock on the stocks page is forecast by a gradient boosted tree model trained on a decade of daily data (December 2016 to October 2026, about 123,000 training rows over 50 symbols), and every stock then improves on its own results:

  • Inputs beyond price history. Each forecast uses 26 features: the stock's own returns, volatility, moving average distances, RSI and volume; the market via SPY, QQQ and the CBOE VIX (level, change and one year percentile); and the stock's sector via its sector ETF. It is not a single price equation.
  • Daily learning loop. After each United States session closes, the engine resolves its pending predictions against what really happened. Each stock keeps its own scorecard and relearns three things daily: a bias correction (rolling mean of its own recent errors), a prediction band width (recalibrated so the stated 80% range keeps its promise), and a per stock blend weight with the offline TimesFM artifact, learned exactly like the NIFTY ensemble weight.
  • Walk forward by year. The training was validated by training one model per year using only earlier years, so every score in the decade table on the stocks page is out of sample.

What honestly improves and what does not. The per stock corrections measurably improve calibration (the 80% range covered 78% of outcomes in a day by day 2026 simulation) and shift direction accuracy by about a point. Next session direction itself stays near 50 to 53% for every fairly tested model on public data, this one included; published academic results cluster at 53 to 58%, and higher claims usually let future information leak into the test. This site will not display a direction accuracy number that its own scorecard has not produced.

The news and events layer (what the models do not read)

Since October 2026 a separate cycle runs at 08:40 IST, before the premarket scan: it collects market headlines (CNBC top news and Google News, United States and India), per stock headlines for all 50 symbols, and the next ten days of earnings dates from the Nasdaq calendar. Headlines are tagged with simple keyword rules (earnings, merger, lawsuit, regulator, layoffs, rates, inflation, geopolitics, election, cyber, recall, accident) and per stock daily headline counts are recorded, so a sudden burst of news is flagged as heavy news. All of it is shown on the dashboard and the stocks page with its as of date and source states.

This layer deliberately stays outside the forecast models. The models would need years of paired news history to learn from headlines honestly, and we will not pretend otherwise: today the news is displayed for you to weigh, and the daily counts accumulate in a blob so a future model version can be trained on them for real.

Sources & integrity policy

  • CNBC: quote.cnbc.com/quote-html-webservice/restQuote for .IXIC, .DJI, .SPX class symbols.
  • Yahoo Finance: chart v8 (^INDIAVIX, ^NSEI, context symbols) and spark v7 (breadth constituents, at most 20 symbols per call).
  • NSE India: market status and advance decline endpoints, tried first for breadth; frequently blocks datacenter IPs, hence the Yahoo fallback.
  • TradingView: GIFT Nifty daily bar close and change from the public symbol page (server rendered state), the only reliable route found from cloud functions.

Known blocked sources (investing.com, nseindia.com pages from cloud IPs, moneycontrol, Alpha Vantage free tier) are not silently substituted: when the primary fails, the row shows the fallback source that actually answered, or the word unavailable. Prices may be delayed by up to 15 to 20 minutes depending on the provider.

FAQ

When exactly is the report generated?

Twice per trading day: 09:00 IST (premarket report) and 09:20 IST (post open confirmation appended to the same report). Manual runs from the dashboard button are possible any time and are rate limited to one per two minutes.

What happens on weekends and exchange holidays?

Weekends are skipped by the scheduler (in IST, not UTC). Exchange holidays are not modelled: the scan still runs and reports the latest available session values, each labelled with its own session date, so nothing pretends to be “today's” data.

Why do some checks show “unavailable” instead of a number?

Because the source failed or blocked the request. The check is then excluded from the score. Inventing or carrying forward a stale value silently would be worse than admitting the gap.

Can I get the raw data?

Yes: /api/latest (JSON of the newest report), /api/history (index of saved dates), /api/report?date=YYYY-MM-DD&format=json|md|html for any archived day, and /assets/model-analysis.json for the current LSTM vs TimesFM comparison.

Is this investment advice?

No. It is a research tool showing what public data said before the open, with sources, timestamps and honest error bars. Do your own diligence.

How often is the model retrained?

Weights ship frozen with the deployment. The documented retraining procedure (same script, refreshed NIFTY CSV, repack) is part of the project; the dashboard flags when prices drift out of the trained scaler range, which is the trigger to retrain.
